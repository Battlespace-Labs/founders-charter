# AIexandria Foundation 0004 — Universe of Reality and Knowledge

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Draft conceptual model |
| Classification | [Epistemological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) foundation |

## Principle

UTOPIA models two coupled but distinct domains:

- the **Universe of Reality (UoR)**, in which phenomena occur or hold;
- the **Universe of Knowledge (UoK)**, in which observations, assertions, evidence, confidence, disagreement, and consensus evolve.

Assertions bridge these domains. They live in the UoK and refer toward proposed features of the UoR.

## Universe of Reality

The UoR denotes reality itself. Ontologically, what happened does not change because an analyst later learns more, changes an attribution, or publishes a correction. Reality may nevertheless be only partially observable, uncertain to us, and unknowable in full.

UTOPIA must therefore avoid treating a stored model as reality.

## Universe of Knowledge

The UoK contains the evolving intellectual and evidentiary history of observers and communities:

```text
unaware → observation → suspicion → hypothesis → supported assertion
        → increased confidence → counterevidence → revision → consensus or rejection
```

This history is not merely a sequence of database versions. It records who knew or believed what, when, on which evidence, in what context, and according to which method.

Different observers and Communities of Trust may occupy different UoK states at the same time.

## Modeled reality: UoRᵐ

Because the UoR is not directly available as a complete dataset, operational systems work with a model:

- **UoR** — reality itself, fixed in the ontological sense and incompletely knowable;
- **UoK** — the evolving body of observations and claims;
- **UoRᵐ(c, k)** — a reconstruction of reality generated from a selected context or community `c` at knowledge time `k`.

UoRᵐ is a projection derived from the UoK. It changes as evidence, methods, trust, and assertions change. There may be multiple valid projections because context and community selections differ.

## Two coupled histories

An assertion can carry at least two independent temporal scopes:

1. **Phenomenological or reality scope:** when the asserted phenomenon is believed to have occurred or held.
2. **Epistemic or knowledge scope:** when the assertion was made, available, accepted, challenged, revised, or superseded.

Example:

```text
Claim: Initial compromise occurred between 01:30 and 02:30 on March 14.

Reality scope claimed:     March 14, 01:30–02:30
Observed:                  March 14, 02:27
Detected as an incident:   March 14, 03:02
First asserted:            March 14, 08:45
Published:                 March 14, 14:20
Received by partner:       March 14, 18:30
Accepted by partner:       March 14, 19:15
Superseded:                April 2
```

The compromise did not change on April 2. The knowledge claim changed.

## Operational significance

The distinction supports different questions:

- What do we currently believe happened on March 14?
- What did analysts believe on March 14?
- What evidence was available to a decision-maker at a specific time?
- How did attribution confidence evolve?
- Which community accepted or rejected the assertion, and when?
- Which later knowledge must be excluded to evaluate a historical decision fairly?

These questions are essential for incident reconstruction, intelligence assessment, audit, lessons learned, and explainable automated reasoning.

## Community of Trust

Knowledge is not globally uniform. A Community of Trust provides a context for:

- membership and identity;
- evidence access;
- source evaluation;
- confidence and acceptance rules;
- dissemination policy;
- shared or contested doctrine.

Community acceptance is itself an assertion with provenance and time. Consensus must not be converted silently into objective reality.

## Ontological cautions

- “Reality is fixed” is an [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) commitment, not a claim that reality is fully accessible.
- UoR and UoK are not necessarily two physical graphs or databases.
- “Dual manifolds” may be a useful metaphor, but it is not yet a formal mathematical model.
- UoRᵐ is not singular; projections depend on context, evidence policy, and knowledge time.
- Assertions do not create reality merely because they are authoritative or widely accepted.

## Open questions

- Can the UoR/UoK distinction be formalized without unnecessary metaphysical commitments?
- What precisely constitutes a UoR referent for inferred, social, or abstract phenomena?
- How are multiple Communities of Trust compared or merged?
- What is the formal relationship between evidence, assertion, belief, acceptance, and consensus?
- How should unknown, unknowable, and deliberately withheld knowledge differ?
- When is a UoRᵐ projection reproducible, and what inputs must be retained?
